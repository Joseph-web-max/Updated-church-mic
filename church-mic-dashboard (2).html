      📖 How to use this dashboard

      A. Using the laptop speakers (no amplifier)

      1. Click 💻 Laptop Speakers. The volume is limited to 60% for safety.
      2. Click 🎤 Turn Mic ON and choose Allow when the browser asks for the microphone.
      3. In Microphone, choose your microphone. In Speakers, check it says Speakers (Realtek(R) Audio).
      4. Press 🔊 Test speakers. If you hear the tone, the output works.
      5. Speak and watch the Microphone level bar move. Keep it mostly in the green.
      6. Click 📡 Go ON AIR to send your voice to the speakers.

      B. Using the church amplifier

      1. Plug the laptop’s headphone jack into the amplifier’s AUX / LINE IN with an audio cable. Turn the amplifier volume down first.
      2. Click 🔌 Amplifier, then 🎤 Turn Mic ON.
      3. In Speakers, choose the headphone or line-out output, then press Test speakers and raise the amplifier volume slowly.
      4. Click 📡 Go ON AIR and set the loudness with the Speaker volume slider.

      C. USB hub and wireless microphone receiver

      1. Plug the wireless receiver into the laptop with its USB cable, or through a powered USB hub. Switch the receiver and the microphones on.
      2. Click 🔗 USB Hub / Wireless Receiver, then 🎤 Turn Mic ON.
      3. In Microphone 1, choose the receiver. It often shows as “USB Audio Device”.
      4. Two microphones on one receiver: if the receiver shows as one input, tick Receiver gives 2 microphones on one stereo input. If it shows as two inputs, choose the second one in Microphone 2.
      5. Use the Mic 1 and Mic 2 sliders to balance the two voices, and the Mute buttons to silence one of them.
      6. Send the sound to the amplifier through a USB sound card, or a hub with a headphone or line-out jack, plugged into AUX / LINE IN. Choose that device in Speakers.
      About batteries: the wireless microphones still need their own batteries (rechargeable ones work well), because the software cannot power them. The receiver and the laptop can run from mains power through the hub, and keeping the laptop plugged in saves its battery. A plain USB hub only adds ports and does not carry sound, so you need a USB sound card or a hub with audio built in. If your receiver only has XLR or 6.35 mm outputs and no USB, put a small USB audio interface between the receiver and the laptop.

      The picture strip at the top

      • 🎤 ➜ 💻 ➜ 🔌 ➜ 🔊 shows the path of your voice. Green means that step is connected or working, orange means ready, and red means muted. The arrows move while you are live.
      • Click the pictures: 🎤 turns the microphone on or off, 🔌 jumps to the output list, and 🔊 plays a test tone.

      Microphone battery

      • Tick The microphone is charging while in use when the microphone has its charger or USB cable connected. The dashboard then shows “charging while in use” and stops counting down.
      • Without a charger, it estimates the time left from the hours you enter and the time you have been ON AIR. Press Battery is full now after charging or changing the battery.
      • Read battery by Bluetooth shows the real battery level if the microphone shares it. Many do not, and then the hours estimate is used.
      • You get a warning at about 20% and a stronger one at about 10%. These are estimates, so keep spare batteries ready.

      System Guardian (automatic safety)

      • The Guardian watches four things: the laptop’s power, the microphone, the selected output, and whether sound is arriving.
      • If the charger is unplugged or mains power fails, the microphone is lost (for example the receiver loses power), the selected speaker or amplifier output is unplugged, or the battery gets critically low, it fades the sound out in a quarter of a second and disconnects. The fade prevents a loud pop in the speakers.
      • Only this dashboard’s own audio connections are closed. Windows settings, the laptop, a phone and every other device are left alone and keep working.
      • It never reconnects by itself. When the problem is fixed, press Turn Mic ON and Go ON AIR. The sound then fades in gently.
      • The power setting lets you choose: disconnect at once, disconnect when the battery is below 15%, or warn only. Use Test safe disconnect to see what happens before a service.
      • If no sound comes from the microphone for 10 seconds while you are ON AIR, a warning appears. Check the microphone’s battery and the receiver.
      • Limits: a web page cannot see the amplifier’s own mains supply. It works from the laptop’s power, device unplugging and missing sound. For real protection from power cuts and surges, plug the amplifier and receiver into a surge protector or a UPS. Power status works in Chrome and Edge only.

      Security

      • The dashboard asks for a PIN when it opens and after 10 minutes without use (never while you are ON AIR). Create a 4 to 8 digit PIN the first time.
      • Click 🔒 Lock to lock it at any time. If it is locked while ON AIR, the Emergency Mute button stays on the lock screen.
      • Five wrong PINs lock it for 30 seconds.
      • Everything stays on this laptop. Nothing is sent over the internet, and a recording is only saved when you download it.
      • If the PIN is forgotten, clear this site’s data in the browser settings, which also removes the saved settings. The PIN keeps casual users out but does not stop someone who can clear the browser data, so also protect the laptop with a Windows password.

      During the service

      • EMERGENCY MUTE (or press M or Esc) silences the speakers instantly. Press it again to unmute.
      • 📡 Go OFF AIR stops sending your voice. 🎤 Turn Mic OFF releases the microphone completely.
      • ⏺ Record Sermon starts recording. Press it again to stop, then click the download link that appears.
      • Keep the page open and the laptop plugged in. Do not close the browser tab while preaching.

      If something is wrong

      • Level bar moves but no sound: check the pill at the top says ON AIR, not MUTED. Press Test speakers, then check the Speakers list and the volume slider.
      • Level bar does not move: choose another microphone in the list, and check the microphone is on, charged and paired in Windows.
      • Screeching (feedback): lower the volume, move the microphone away from the speakers, or press Emergency Mute. The Feedback guard also lowers the volume by itself.
      • Voice sounds thin or delayed: this is common with Bluetooth microphones. A wired or USB microphone sounds better.
      • Microphone blocked: click the padlock next to the page address, allow the microphone, and reload the page.
      Before every service: arrive early, do a short test with someone listening at the back, and use Chrome or Edge.
      Using the laptop alone (no amplifier)? Sound plays from the laptop speakers, or from headphones if plugged in. Use the Test speakers button to check the sound, and wear headphones while testing, because a microphone close to laptop speakers causes screeching.
      Avoid feedback (screeching): keep the microphone away from the speakers and start with the volume low, then raise it slowly. Connect the laptop’s headphone output to the amplifier’s AUX / LINE IN with a cable, and set Windows to use that output.
      Bluetooth microphones may sound thinner and add a small delay. For best quality use a wired or USB microphone, or the microphone’s own USB receiver.
